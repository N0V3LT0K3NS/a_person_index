# Divergent Association Task

## What it is
A brief creativity task where participants generate words that should be as unrelated as possible, then receive a score based on the average semantic distance among those words.

## Why it matters
DAT adds a modern, automation-friendly divergent-thinking measure to the canonical registry. It contributes a narrow but useful creativity-performance seam that complements broader trait frameworks and symbolic systems.

## How it is administered and scored
- participants provide 10 words intended to be maximally different
- implementations keep the first valid subset of responses for scoring
- semantic distance is computed pairwise across the retained words
- the DAT score is a transformed average distance score, where higher values indicate more remote association structure in responses

## Validity and limits
Initial studies report convergent validity with established creativity tasks and meaningful short-term test-retest reliability. At the same time, DAT remains a constrained task measure: strategy choice, language factors, and instruction adherence can shift scores.

## What it is good for
- scalable divergent-thinking measurement in research contexts
- low-overhead creativity-task baselining
- complementing broader multi-framework synthesis when used conservatively

## What it is weaker at
- stable personality interpretation
- whole-person creativity diagnosis
- high-stakes standalone screening decisions

## Common misuse
Treating DAT as equivalent to a broad personality trait or as a complete measure of creativity rather than a narrow divergent-association task signal.
