# Multi-Subject Comparison

A Person Index starts from a single comparative substrate, but that substrate
can support more than one kind of comparison.

This document names the main comparison shapes so they do not all get forced
into one-person mixed-stack intake.

## Why this matters

Comparison can happen:

- within one person across time
- within one person across contexts or roles
- between two or more people
- across a temporary hand-entered group

Those are different tasks, even when they use the same underlying grammar.

## Intra-subject comparison

This is comparison within one person.

Examples:

- 2013 strengths versus 2020 strengths
- work self versus romantic self
- role A versus role B
- symbolic stack versus psychometric stack for the same person

This kind of comparison is often the first expansion beyond a basic intake pass.

## Inter-subject comparison

This is comparison across two or more people.

Examples:

- pairwise comparison
- relationship-oriented comparison
- group or team comparison
- side-by-side stack comparison without persistent user accounts

The same comparative grammar can still apply:

- what converges
- what differs
- what is orthogonal
- what creates friction or complementarity

## Contextual comparison

Not every comparison needs a second person.

Sometimes the key comparison is:

- same person, different context

That can be just as meaningful as comparing two people.

## What belongs in the core repo

This repo can define:

- the comparison grammar
- the artifact semantics
- the relevant programs or protocol ideas
- the boundaries and caveats

This repo should not automatically become:

- a multi-user account system
- a persistence layer for linked identities
- a relationship app
- a shared-user data store

## Practical starting point

The safest current shape is ad hoc comparison:

- manually provided result stacks
- context-tagged inputs
- explicit scope
- no hidden identity persistence

That preserves comparative power without prematurely taking on application-layer
complexity.

## Seeded comparison paths

The repo now has two first-class comparison programs for this layer:

- `Contextual Comparison Memo`
- `Pairwise Relational Comparison`

And two seed packs for getting started without assembling everything by hand:

- `Contextual Core Trait-Motive Stack`
- `Pairwise Relational Baseline`

These are intentionally narrow.

They do not claim to solve every multi-subject use case. They give agents a
disciplined way to begin contextual and pairwise work while keeping scope,
missingness, and evidence partitions visible.

## Important caution

Multi-subject comparison intensifies all the normal risks:

- overreading weak correspondences
- flattening different framework layers together
- hiding missingness
- turning the output into one smooth story

The same discipline that matters for one-person work matters even more here.
